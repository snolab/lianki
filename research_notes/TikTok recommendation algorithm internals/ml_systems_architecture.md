# ByteDance/TikTok recommendation ML systems architecture: Monolith and later published systems

Scope: how the recommendation model is built, trained and served at ByteDance scale, as described in ByteDance's own publications (Monolith 2022, Primus ATC'25, RankMixer KDD'25, LONGER RecSys'25, TokenMixer-Large 2026, Trinity KDD'24, the Douyin 10K-sequence paper), ByteDance's commercial docs (BytePlus Recommend, Volcano Engine), and TikTok's official explainer, with third-party material labelled. State of knowledge as of October 2026.

Labelling convention used below:
- **[ByteDance-primary]** = stated by ByteDance/TikTok/BytePlus/Volcano Engine itself (paper, docs, newsroom, confirmed leak).
- **[Third-party]** = press or blog interpretation, not confirmed by ByteDance.
- **[Inference]** = my own reasoning from the cited material.

## Key question 1: What exactly does the Monolith paper (Liu et al., 2022, arXiv 2209.07663) describe, with concrete numbers?

### Takeaway
Monolith is ByteDance's TensorFlow-based production recommendation training/serving system whose two contributions are (a) a collisionless, Cuckoo-hash embedding table with frequency filtering and expiry to keep multi-terabyte sparse models tractable, and (b) an online-training loop in which a training parameter server (PS) consumes a Kafka/Flink joined stream and pushes sparse-embedding updates to the serving PS at minute-level, dense updates at day-level, trading snapshot-based fault tolerance for freshness. On Criteo, online training with a 30-minute sync beats batch by ~0.37 AUC points (79.80 vs 79.43); in a live ads A/B test the paper reports 14–18% "AUC improvement" per day over seven days.

### Cited Findings
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Authors: Zhuoran Liu, Leqi Zou, Xuan Zou, Caihua Wang, Biao Zhang, Da Tang, Yijie Zhu, Peng Wu, Ke Wang, Youlong Cheng (corresponding) of ByteDance Inc.; Bolin Zhu of Fudan University (ByteDance intern). — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Collisionless table = Cuckoo HashMap: "maintains two tables T0, T1 with different hash functions h0(x), h1(x)"; "worst-case O(1) time complexity for lookups and deletions, and an expected amortized O(1) time for insertions"; a colliding element is evicted from T0 and inserted into T1, repeating until stable or a rehash. — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Frequency filtering: IDs are filtered "by their occurrences before they are inserted as keys, where the threshold of occurrences is a tunable hyperparameter"; a probabilistic filter is also offered to cut memory. — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Expirable embeddings: "IDs are timed and set to expire after being inactive for a predefined period of time. The expire time is also tunable." — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Two training stages: batch training reads mini-batches from storage; in online training "training worker consumes realtime data on-the-fly". Pipeline: Kafka queues for user actions and features, a Flink streaming job acting as the online joiner that "concatenates features with labels from user actions and produces training examples", using an in-memory cache plus on-disk key-value store for delayed/late features. — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Negative sampling in the joiner is compensated with "log odds correction during serving, making sure that the online model is an unbiased estimator of the original distribution". — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Parameter sync: sparse parameters synced at "minute-level"; dense parameters at "day-level" because "Dense variables move much slower than sparse embeddings", scheduled "when the traffic is lowest (e.g. midnight)". A hash set of "touched keys" (IDs trained since last sync) limits what is pushed. Bandwidth example given: "100,000 IDs gets updated in a minute, and the dimension of embedding is 1024, the total size of data need to be transferred is 4KB x 100,000 ≈ 400MB per minute". — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Serving-side updates are "incremental on-the-fly periodic parameter synchronization" because replacing a "multi-terabyte" model wholesale is infeasible; updates "must be made on-the-fly" without stopping service. — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Fault tolerance: "snapshots all training PS every day". Argument quoted: "Suppose a model's parameters are sharded across 1000 PS, and they snapshot every day. Given 0.01% failure rate, one of them will go down every 10 days and we lose all updates on this PS for 1 day. Assuming a DAU of 15 Million and an even distribution of user IDs on each PS, we lose 1 day's feedback from 15000 users every 10 days." — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Scale: "Models in production is usually several terabytes in size"; the internal production dataset has "around 1000 embedding tables" with a hashed ID space baseline of "2^48 decomposed to 2^25"; daily feature updates are "very uneven" across tables. — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** MovieLens-25M experiment (DeepFM; ~162k users, 62k movies): collision rates under hashing were 7.73% for user IDs and 2.86% for movie IDs; collisionless embedding "consistently outperforms those with collision". On the internal production data the collisionless model "has higher AUC from the first epoch and converges at higher value". — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Criteo experiment: 7 days of chronological data, "5 days of data for batch training, and 2 days for online training". Table 2 (AUC, online vs batch): sync interval 5 hr: 79.66±0.020 vs 79.42±0.026; 1 hr: 79.78±0.005 vs 79.44±0.030; 30 min: 79.80±0.008 vs 79.43±0.025. — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Live experiment "compares online training to batch training on one of our Ads model in production"; Table 3 reports per-day improvements of 14.443, 16.871, 17.068, 14.028, 18.081, 16.404, 15.202 (%) over 7 days. — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Monolith was open-sourced at github.com/bytedance/monolith: TensorFlow-based, Bazel 3.1.0 build, Linux only, with distributed-async-training tutorials under `/markdown/demo`; the fetched README reports the repository as archived (as of October 2025). ~9.3k stars, 721 forks. — [bytedance/monolith GitHub](https://github.com/bytedance/monolith)
- **[Third-party]** The Next Platform's coverage adds claims not in the paper: that Monolith would be released through BytePlus Recommend, and speculates it "would be a significant boon to the company's video-centric TikTok platform"; the paper itself only says deployment "on some of the most important products". — [The Next Platform](https://www.nextplatform.com/2022/09/26/chinas-bytedance-intros-different-approach-to-recommendation-at-scale/)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** The Table 3 numbers (14–18%) are far larger than the Criteo AUC deltas (~0.4 points) and are presumably a relative metric on a production ads model; the paper's table is labelled as "AUC improvement" but readers should treat it as a relative/online metric rather than an absolute AUC delta. The paper's text supports only "online training beats batch training on a production ads model every day for 7 days".
- **[Inference]** The "DAU of 15 Million" in the fault-tolerance argument is an illustrative assumption for one model, not a disclosure of a product's DAU.
- **[Inference]** The 400 MB/min bandwidth example implies the touched-key set, not the full table, is the unit of sync, so per-minute sync cost scales with activity rather than model size.

### Gaps
- The paper does not state the production sync interval actually used, only that sparse sync is "minute-level" and that 30-min/1-hr/5-hr were tested offline.
- The paper does not give the serving-side latency budget, candidate counts, or GPU/CPU serving hardware.
- No ByteDance statement ties Monolith specifically to TikTok (vs. Douyin, Toutiao, ads); only "some of the most important products".
- Precise frequency-filter threshold and expiry values used in production are not disclosed (described as tunable).

## Key question 2: How do training and serving PS exchange updates, and how fast does a user interaction influence their next recommendation?

### Takeaway
In Monolith the loop is: user action -> Kafka -> Flink online joiner (with cache for late labels) -> training worker/training PS -> touched-key sparse push to serving PS at minute granularity, dense weights nightly; so a model-level influence on recommendations is minute-scale, not sub-second. ByteDance's commercial BytePlus Recommend states a 10-second data-to-model path for events sent via its Write APIs, and later ByteDance papers (LONGER, 2025) describe moving to a fully synchronous GPU-resident training/serving design that removes the external PS altogether.

### Cited Findings
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Monolith's online loop: Kafka user-action and feature queues -> Flink online joiner -> training worker consuming real-time data -> training PS; serving PS "syncs its parameters with the training-PS periodically", with sparse updates at "minute-level" and dense at "day-level" (midnight). — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Only "touched keys" (IDs whose embeddings were trained since last sync) are pushed; e.g. 100k IDs/min at dim 1024 ≈ 400 MB/min. — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Monolith explicitly trades reliability for freshness: daily PS snapshots instead of frequent ones, accepting loss of "1 day's feedback from 15000 users every 10 days" under the 1000-PS/0.01%-failure assumption. — [Monolith, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.07663)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** BytePlus Recommend docs: "if data is sent to us via WriteAPIs, it will be fed into the model after 10 seconds and reflected to the next recommendation call"; sending real-time updates via WriteAPIs is recommended "for the greatest real-time model accuracy". — [BytePlus Recommend docs: Ways to improve recommendation performance](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/recommend/resources-ways_to_improve_recommendation_performance)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** BytePlus Recommend monitoring exposes API latency for Data Synchronization, Predict and AckServerImpression APIs; the default alert threshold is API latency above 300 ms. — [BytePlus Recommend API Monitoring Dashboard](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/recommend/docs-api_monitoring_dashboard)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Primus (USENIX ATC '25) "devises a new hybrid training paradigm for DLRMs that ensures high model timeliness by controlling parameter updates and applying fine-grained prioritization of mixed batch and stream data"; deployed "five years" at ByteDance. — [Primus, USENIX ATC '25](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc25/presentation/shan-jixi)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** ByteDance's ApacheCon Asia 2023 talk (Mao Hongyue, infra engineer for Douyin/Toutiao/ads training engines) describes a batch-stream integrated training framework (open-sourced as Primus) mixing Iceberg/HDFS batch data with Kafka streams; infrastructure scale cited: 10,000+ daily jobs, 5 million CPU-core tasks, 10,000 GPU-card tasks, ~500 TB average training data per task. — [ApacheCon Asia 2023 session](https://apachecon.com/acasia2023/sessions/ai-1086.html)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** LONGER (RecSys '25) describes "the fully synchronous model training and serving framework for unified GPU-based dense and sparse parameter updates": "Both dense and sparse parameters are stored and updated synchronously on GPU machines, eliminating the need for external Parameter Server components", with hierarchical embedding storage (HBM for high-frequency, CPU memory for mid-frequency, SSD for low-frequency features). — [LONGER, arXiv 2505.04421](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04421)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** TikTok's official explainer says the For You feed is driven by user interactions, video information and device/account settings, with strong signals (finishing a longer video) weighted above weak ones (same country). — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** For a Monolith-style deployment, the floor on "interaction -> changed model weights in serving" is the sparse sync interval (minutes) plus joiner delay; the paper's offline ablation shows diminishing returns between 1 hr and 30 min (79.78 -> 79.80 AUC), consistent with minute-level being "good enough" rather than sub-minute.
- **[Inference]** Immediate within-session adaptation that users perceive (e.g. the next few videos reacting to a skip) is more plausibly driven by real-time features (recent-behavior sequences, counters) fed at request time than by weight updates; ByteDance's sequence-modeling papers (LONGER, 10K-sequence) are consistent with this but no primary source states the split explicitly.
- **[Inference]** The BytePlus "10 seconds" figure is for the SaaS product and most likely reflects real-time feature/event ingestion rather than full model-weight updates; it should not be read as Monolith's production sync interval.
- **[Inference]** The LONGER framework's "no external PS" design suggests that by 2025 at least some ByteDance GPU-based ranking models no longer use the Monolith training-PS/serving-PS split, though the paper does not describe how serving replicas receive updates.

### Gaps
- No primary source states the end-to-end latency (seconds/minutes) from a Douyin/TikTok interaction to a weight update being live in production.
- No ByteDance source describes how the synchronous GPU framework in LONGER propagates parameters to serving replicas.
- Volcano Engine's product docs (docs.volcengine.com/docs/6465/1158259) are a JS-rendered app and could not be fetched; cadence numbers for the Volcano Engine recommendation platform are therefore missing here.

## Key question 3: What later ByteDance papers/blog posts describe the ranking model (multi-task, multi-objective, cross networks, user sequence modeling)?

### Takeaway
Since 2024 ByteDance has published a coherent line of ranking-model work: Trinity (KDD '24, retrieval-stage long-term interest clustering on Douyin), RankMixer (KDD '25, a hardware-aware token-mixing architecture that replaced DCN/DHEN/FM-style hand-crafted feature crossing and scaled Douyin's ranking model 100x in parameters at constant latency), LONGER (RecSys '25, 10K-length user-sequence Transformer with KV-cache serving), the Douyin 10K-sequence paper (Nov 2025, STCA cross-attention + request-level batching), and TokenMixer-Large (Feb 2026, 7B online / 15B offline parameters). ByteDance work on short-video search confirms MMoE-style multi-task heads; a leaked, TikTok-confirmed internal doc gives the multi-objective score form P_like*V_like + P_comment*V_comment + E_playtime*V_playtime + P_play*V_play.

### Cited Findings
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Trinity (KDD '24; Jing Yan, Liu Jiang, Jianfei Cui, Zhichen Zhao, Xingyan Bin, Feng Zhang, Zuotao Liu, ByteDance): addresses "interest amnesia" in the retrieval stage; builds "a real-time clustering system that projects items into enumerable clusters and calculates statistical interest histograms over these clusters" to surface under-delivered themes and stay stable under emerging hot topics; derived retrievers "deployed on the recommender system of Douyin", improving retention. — [Trinity, arXiv 2402.02842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02842)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** RankMixer (KDD '25, ByteDance): "most human-designed feature-crossing modules in ranking models were inherited from the CPU era and fail to exploit modern GPUs, resulting in low Model Flops Utilization (MFU)"; replaces quadratic self-attention with multi-head token mixing and per-token FFNs; Sparse-MoE variant with dynamic routing; "boost the model MFU from 4.5% to 45%, and scale our ranking model parameters by 100x while maintaining roughly the same inference latency"; 1B dense-parameter RankMixer launched at full traffic "without increasing the serving cost", improving user active days by 0.3% and total in-app usage duration by 1.08%. — [RankMixer, arXiv 2507.15551](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15551)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** RankMixer details: Douyin recommendation training data of "trillions of records per day"; over 300 features (numerical, ID, cross, sequential); billions of user IDs and hundreds of millions of video IDs; baseline 16M-parameter production model at 14.5 ms inference vs RankMixer ~14.3 ms after a ~70x parameter increase, using fp16 inference and quantization; AUC gains vs baseline on Finish/Skip tasks: 100M variant +0.64%/+0.86%, 1B variant +0.95%/+1.25%; Wukong +0.29% and DHEN +0.18% as comparators; replaced DCN, DHEN and FM-based handcrafted modules; low-activity users showed the largest online gain (+1.74% active days). — [RankMixer HTML, arXiv 2507.15551](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15551)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** LONGER (RecSys '25, all authors ByteDance; corresponding author Yuchao Zheng): global token for attention stability, token merge with lightweight InnerTransformers and hybrid attention, mixed-precision training with activation recomputation, KV-cache serving; "fully deployed at more than 10 influential scenarios at ByteDance, serving billion users". Offline AUC 0.85290 vs base 0.83968 (+1.57% relative) on a 5.2B-sample dataset; token merge K=4 cuts FLOPs 42.8% (587M -> 336M at L=2048, d=32); KV cache serving reduces throughput degradation "from −40% to only −6.8%"; online: Douyin Ads +1.06–2.10% ADSS across live-streaming/short-video/mall; Douyin e-commerce +4.6–7.9% Order/U. — [LONGER, arXiv 2505.04421](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04421)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** "Make It Long, Keep It Fast" (Nov 2025, Douyin): scales end-to-end long-sequence modeling to 10K-length histories in production via Stacked Target-to-History Cross Attention (STCA, linear instead of quadratic in sequence length), Request Level Batching (sharing user-side encoding across multiple targets in one request), and length-extrapolative training (train short, infer long); observes "predictable, monotonic gains as we scale history length and model capacity, mirroring the scaling law behavior observed in large language models"; "Deployed at full traffic on Douyin ... while meeting production latency". — [arXiv 2511.06077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06077)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** TokenMixer-Large (Feb 2026, ByteDance): evolves RankMixer with mixing-and-reverting, inter-layer residuals, an auxiliary loss for gradient stability at depth, and Sparse Per-token MoE; "scales to 7 billion parameters for online traffic and 15 billion in offline experiments"; deployed in multiple ByteDance scenarios with +1.66% orders and +2.98% per-capita spending (e-commerce). — [TokenMixer-Large, arXiv 2602.06563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06563)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Primus (ATC '25): unified resource abstraction across schedulers with horizontal/vertical dynamic scaling; three-tier data definition with data-task-graph generation for multi-source batch+stream samples; results: 17.1% cluster-level training-cost reduction, per-job CPU utilization 50% -> 80%, data task generation 23x faster, advertising revenue +0.4%–2.4% across four DLRMs. — [Primus, USENIX ATC '25](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc25/presentation/shan-jixi); open-source repo at [github.com/bytedance/primus](https://github.com/bytedance/primus)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** ByteDance's short-video search paper ("Beyond Relevance", 2024) states it adopts "the widely-used MMOE architecture to simultaneously learn multiple user behavior feedback" — direct evidence of MMoE-style multi-task heads in a ByteDance short-video ranking stack (search, not the feed). — [arXiv 2409.11281](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.11281)
- **[ByteDance-primary, via confirmed leak]** The internal "TikTok Algo 101" document (authenticity confirmed by TikTok spokeswoman Hilary McQuaide to the NYT) gives the ranking score as "Plike x Vlike + Pcomment x Vcomment + Eplaytime x Vplaytime + Pplay x Vplay", states the system optimizes for retention and time spent, and names four aims: user value, long-term user value, creator value, platform value. — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/leaked-tiktok-doc-reveals-its-obvious-secret-to-an-addi-1848166901); [PopSci](https://www.popsci.com/technology/tiktok-algorithm/)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** TikTok Newsroom: follower count and prior high-performing videos are not direct ranking factors; the system avoids "two videos in a row made with the same sound or by the same creator"; new users pick interest categories or get "a generalized feed of popular videos". — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[Third-party]** Searches for ByteDance-authored multi-objective score-fusion/ensemble papers surfaced only Kuaishou and academic work (HarmonRank, UMRE); no ByteDance paper on the final fusion stage was found. — [arXiv 2601.02955](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02955); [arXiv 2508.07613](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07613)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** The Algo 101 formula is a multi-task, multi-objective fusion: per-objective predicted probabilities/expectations (P_like, P_comment, E_playtime, P_play) weighted by business values (V_*). Combined with the MMoE evidence and RankMixer's "Finish"/"Skip" tasks, the Douyin/TikTok ranker is almost certainly a multi-task model with a hand- or learned-weighted fusion; the exact fusion mechanism is undisclosed.
- **[Inference]** ByteDance's 2025–2026 papers show a deliberate architectural shift from CPU-era feature-crossing modules (DCN/DHEN/FM) toward Transformer-like, GPU-efficient token models with LLM-style scaling laws, and from short sequence windows toward 10K-length end-to-end histories.
- **[Inference]** Trinity operates in retrieval; RankMixer/TokenMixer-Large/LONGER operate in ranking. Together they imply the canonical multi-stage pipeline (retrieval -> ranking -> fusion/rerank) even though no single ByteDance paper lays out the whole Douyin pipeline.

### Gaps
- No ByteDance publication found that describes the full Douyin/TikTok feed pipeline end to end (candidate counts per stage, pre-ranking model, final rerank).
- The Algo 101 formula is from a non-technical internal explainer (2021); no primary source gives the current objective set or weights.
- "Large Foundation Model for Ads Recommendation" (arXiv 2508.14948) and "Short Video Segment-level User Dynamic Interests" (arXiv 2504.04237) appeared in results but their ByteDance affiliation was not verified here.
- No ByteDance paper on embedding compression specific to Douyin was located; "Mixed-Precision Embeddings" (arXiv 2409.20305, ~200x compression on Criteo) appeared in search but its affiliation was not confirmed as ByteDance.

## Key question 4: What is publicly known about the serving stack (latency budgets, candidate counts, GPU inference) from ByteDance talks, Volcano Engine or BytePlus docs?

### Takeaway
Concrete serving numbers from ByteDance itself are sparse: RankMixer gives a ~14 ms ranking-model inference latency on Douyin with fp16/quantized GPU inference at 1B parameters; LONGER describes KV-cache reuse of user-side computation across candidates; BytePlus Recommend documents a 300 ms API-latency alert default and a 10-second event-to-model path; Volcano Engine says its platform is built on ByteDance's internal recommendation middleware and does end-to-end objective optimization with real-time features and training. Candidate counts per stage for Douyin/TikTok are not disclosed by ByteDance.

### Cited Findings
- **[ByteDance-primary]** RankMixer: production baseline ranking model 16M parameters at 14.5 ms inference; 1B-parameter RankMixer served at ~14.3 ms via fp16 inference (2x theoretical FLOPs) and quantization; "strict latency bounds and high QPS demands" but no QPS figure given. — [RankMixer HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15551)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** LONGER: user-sequence key/value tensors are precomputed once per request and reused across candidate scoring, cutting serving throughput degradation from −40% to −6.8%; sparse/dense parameters colocated on GPU machines with HBM/CPU-memory/SSD tiering. — [LONGER HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04421)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** Douyin 10K-sequence paper: Request Level Batching aggregates multiple targets for the same user/request to share user-side encoding, "substantially lowering sequence-related storage, communication, and compute"; deployed at full Douyin traffic "while meeting production latency" (no ms figure in abstract). — [arXiv 2511.06077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06077)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** BytePlus Recommend: events via WriteAPIs reach the model "after 10 seconds and reflected to the next recommendation call". — [BytePlus docs](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/recommend/resources-ways_to_improve_recommendation_performance)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** BytePlus Recommend monitoring: latency graphs for Data Synchronization, Predict and AckServerImpression APIs; default alert when any API's latency exceeds 300 ms. — [BytePlus docs](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/recommend/docs-api_monitoring_dashboard); API list at [BytePlus Recommend API list](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/recommend/reference-api_list)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** BytePlus Recommend marketing: ingests "terabytes of real-time user behavior and product information"; "designed to support auto-scaling and real-time processing"; no pipeline-stage or latency specifics. — [BytePlus "Under the hood"](https://byteplus.com/en/insights/under-the-hood-with-byteplus-recommend-)
- **[ByteDance-primary, via press quoting Volcano Engine]** Volcano Engine's intelligent recommendation platform "就是基于字节推荐中台打造的" (is built on ByteDance's recommendation middle platform); claims end-to-end optimization of the business objective, real-time feature updates and real-time model training, multi-objective support, black-box and white-box modes, and an e-commerce customer with "人均GMV100%的增长" (100% per-user GMV growth). — [QbitAI, Dec 2021](https://www.qbitai.com/2021/12/30680.html)
- **[Third-party]** A Volcano Engine developer-community article on search uses a generic recall -> rank -> rerank framing and the example that a recall layer first narrows billions of items to "比如800个" (e.g., 800) before coarse and fine ranking; this is an illustrative number for the generic pattern, not a Douyin disclosure. — [Volcano Engine developer article (search result summary)](https://developer.volcengine.com/articles/7317095759994683430)
- **[ByteDance-primary]** ApacheCon Asia 2023: ByteDance training infra handles 10,000+ daily jobs, 5M CPU-core tasks, 10,000 GPU-card tasks, ~500 TB average training data per task. — [ApacheCon Asia 2023](https://apachecon.com/acasia2023/sessions/ai-1086.html)
- **[Third-party]** Third-party GPU-serving papers often cited alongside Monolith (NVIDIA HugeCTR HPS, arXiv 2210.08804; "Pushing the Performance Envelope of DNN-based Recommendation Systems Inference on GPUs", arXiv 2410.22249, which finds the embedding stage causes up to 3.2x slowdown) are not ByteDance work and say nothing about ByteDance's stack. — [arXiv 2210.08804](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.08804); [arXiv 2410.22249](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.22249)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** A ~14 ms ranking-model inference budget (RankMixer) sits inside a larger end-to-end request budget that must also cover retrieval, feature fetch, pre-ranking and rerank; the BytePlus 300 ms alert default is the closest public figure for a full-request SLA, and it applies to the SaaS product, not Douyin.
- **[Inference]** The 2025 papers (GPU-resident sparse parameters, KV-cache reuse, request-level batching) indicate Douyin ranking inference is GPU-based with user-side computation amortised across candidates in a request; this is consistent with, but goes beyond, the Monolith-era CPU parameter-server design.

### Gaps
- No ByteDance source gives Douyin/TikTok candidate counts per stage, total request latency budget, or QPS.
- The Volcano Engine docs page could not be fetched (JS-rendered), so first-party Volcano Engine cadence/latency numbers are missing.
- No ByteDance talk or doc found that describes the pre-ranking model or the final rerank/diversity stage in engineering detail.

## Key question 5: Which parts are confirmed by ByteDance vs inferred by third parties?

### Takeaway
Confirmed by ByteDance: Monolith's design and offline/online results; Primus's batch-stream training and cost numbers; RankMixer/TokenMixer-Large/LONGER/10K-sequence architectures and their Douyin online gains; Trinity's retrieval clustering; MMoE use in short-video search; BytePlus's 10-second event path and 300 ms alert; TikTok's signal categories and the (confirmed-authentic) Algo 101 score formula. Inferred by third parties or by this note: that Monolith specifically powers TikTok; the end-to-end interaction-to-recommendation latency; per-stage candidate counts; the exact multi-objective fusion used today.

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** The strongest publicly supportable architecture statement is: Kafka/Flink real-time joining -> Primus-orchestrated hybrid batch/stream training -> (historically) Monolith PS with minute-level sparse sync; (by 2025) GPU-resident synchronous training; GPU ranking inference of RankMixer-family models with 10K-length user sequences, at ~14 ms model latency, with Trinity-style clustered retrieval upstream and a multi-objective value-weighted fusion downstream.

### Gaps
- Nothing from ByteDance publicly confirms that TikTok (as opposed to Douyin) runs the same ranking models described in the 2025–2026 papers; all online experiments cited are on Douyin, Douyin Ads or Douyin e-commerce.
