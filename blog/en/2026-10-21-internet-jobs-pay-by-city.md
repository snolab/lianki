# What Internet Jobs Pay City by City — and Which Languages Get You There

_Posted October 21, 2026 by [lianki.com](https://lianki.com)_

Our [city ranking](/en/blog/2026-10-21-languages-by-city-wealth) used GDP per person, which says how rich a city is, not what a job there pays. This post uses actual wages, from each country's official statistics, for the kind of work most people mean by "internet jobs": **software, design, media and writing, and marketing.**

The short version: **English opens the best-paid internet jobs by a wide margin, German comes next, and inside bilingual Canada the French-speaking cities pay less than the English-speaking ones.**

---

## How to read the numbers

- **Official sources only** (plus one developer survey, clearly marked): the US Bureau of Labor Statistics, the UK's ONS, Germany's Federal Employment Agency, Statistics Canada, Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, the Australian Bureau of Statistics and Spain's INE.
- **One unit:** everything is **gross annual pay in purchasing-power dollars** — monthly pay × 12, weekly × 52, divided by the World Bank's purchasing-power factor for that country and year. A "$100k" job in Berlin buys about what $100k buys in the US on average. It does not account for rent in that particular city.
- **Median, not average.** The median is the middle earner. Where an agency only publishes averages (French cities, Japanese prefectures) we leave those out of the tables rather than mix them in.
- **P10 / P90** are the earners 10% from the bottom and 10% from the top. Only some agencies publish them for a city or an occupation; "—" means they don't.
- **Not every country measures the same thing.** Each table says what was measured. The biggest differences: Canada's figures are from 2020 and include the self-employed; Japan's leave out bonuses; Australia's and Spain's include part-time workers; Germany's IT group is broader than "software developers".

- **Every US metro in the list pays more than every city outside the US.** San Jose (Silicon Valley) leads at a median of $213k, and even Houston, the lowest US city here, is at $129k.
- **German cities come next.** Munich, Frankfurt and Stuttgart are above London. These are all IT jobs, not only developers, and the true medians are probably higher: German statistics stop counting pay above the pension-contribution ceiling of €8,050 a month, and in Munich about 70% of IT workers earn over €6,000.
- **London is the UK's outlier.** The rest of the country's regions sit between $68k and $80k.
- **Canada looks lowest,** but its numbers are from 2020 — prices and pay have risen a lot since — and they are by place of residence and include the self-employed. Compare Canadian cities with each other, not with the rest of the table.

## How wide the spread is

Only some agencies publish the full distribution for software or IT jobs. Where they do:

| Where                    |   P10 |   P25 | Median |   P75 |   P90 | P90 ÷ P10 | What is measured                                                 |
| ------------------------ | ----: | ----: | -----: | ----: | ----: | --------: | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| United States (national) |  $82k | $105k |  $136k | $172k | $215k |       2.6 | Software developers, 2025                                        |
| San Francisco            | $128k | $164k |  $187k | $220k | $273k |       2.1 | Software developers, 2025                                        |
| London                   |  $55k |  $74k |  $100k | $134k | $168k |       3.1 | IT professionals (SOC 213), 2025                                 |
| Japan (national)         |  $28k |  $32k |   $38k |  $47k |  $59k |       2.1 | Software developers, 2025 — monthly pay × 12, bonus not included |
| Australia (national)     |  $46k |  $60k |   $82k | $107k | $152k |       3.3 | ICT professionals, 2025 — includes part-time                     |
| Spain (national)         |  $42k |  $53k |   $67k |  $88k | $109k |       2.6 | IT professionals, 2022 — includes part-time                      |

- **San Francisco is not just higher, it is tighter**: even its bottom 10% of developers earn $128k. In London the bottom 10% of IT professionals earn $55k — less than half of San Francisco's bottom 10%.
- **Japan is the surprise.** The median software developer earns about $38k in purchasing-power terms before bonuses. Bonuses average about ¥1.1 million a year for this job, which adds roughly $11k — still far below the other rich countries.
- **Australia and London have the widest spread** (P90 is more than three times P10), so in those places the job you land matters more than the city.

## Bilingual cities: does the second language pay?

The cleanest test is to compare cities **in the same country, from the same source and year**.

**Canada — French-speaking cities pay less.** Statistics Canada, 2020, full-year full-time workers:

| City            | Languages       | All full-time workers | Software & web developers | Information and cultural industries |
| --------------- | --------------- | --------------------: | ------------------------: | ----------------------------------: |
| Toronto         | English         |                  $54k |                      $74k |                                $65k |
| Vancouver       | English         |                  $52k |                      $78k |                                $65k |
| Ottawa–Gatineau | English, French |                  $58k |                      $74k |                                $66k |
| Montréal        | French, English |                  $48k |                      $66k |                                $58k |
| Québec          | French          |                  $48k |                      $57k |                                $56k |

Montreal is Canada's biggest bilingual city, and its software developers earn about **10% less** than Toronto's. Quebec City, which is almost entirely French-speaking, is lower again. Ottawa–Gatineau matches Toronto; it is the seat of the federal government, the region's largest employer, where French–English bilingual staff are in demand.

## What this means for learners

For internet jobs specifically:

1. **English is the first language to learn** — every one of the highest-paying cities in this post works in English.
2. **German is the strongest second choice** for tech: Munich, Frankfurt and Stuttgart pay above London, and the official medians understate them.
3. **Hebrew, Dutch and the Swiss languages** go with high pay, but in small markets.
4. **French on its own pays less** than you might expect in tech: Montreal and Quebec City trail Toronto, and France's developers sit around the European middle in the survey. It is most valuable together with English, in bilingual cities.
5. **Japanese is worth learning for Japan itself, not for the pay**: official salaries for developers there are far below other rich countries.

Whatever you pick, the way to get to working fluency is the same: lots of real reading and listening, and steady review so you keep what you learn. That is what [Lianki](/en) is for.
