# Which Languages Open the Richest Cities? A City-by-City Ranking

_Posted October 21, 2026 by [lianki.com](https://lianki.com)_

> **This post corrects our earlier [Power Law of Languages](/en/blog/2025-02-24-language-gdp-coverage) post.** That one added up whole countries' GDP per language, and its numbers had mistakes, listed under "What was wrong with the country version" below.

If you are learning a language to improve your career, the size of a country's economy is not what matters to you. You don't get a job "in India" or "in France" — you get one in Bengaluru or in Paris. And what matters for one person is how much a job there is worth, not how many jobs exist in total.

So this time we counted **cities**, and ranked them by **GDP per person** — how much economic value the city produces for each resident. Then we listed which languages you can live and work in at each one.

---

## How we did it

- **Cities:** all 499 urban centres with at least 1 million people in 2020, from the EU Joint Research Centre's [GHS Urban Centre Database](https://human-settlement.emergency.copernicus.eu/ghs_ucdb_2024.php) (R2024A). It draws every city's boundary the same way, from satellite data, so a "city" means the same thing in Tokyo and in Lagos.
- **Value per job:** GDP per person in 2020, in purchasing-power dollars (PPP, so a dollar buys roughly the same everywhere). There is no comparable dataset of median wages for cities worldwide, so GDP per person is the closest stand-in. It is not the same as what a job pays — more on that below. For real wages in software, design, media and marketing jobs, see [What Internet Jobs Pay City by City](/en/blog/2026-10-21-internet-jobs-pay-by-city).
- **Languages:** for each city, the languages people use for daily life and work there. **Bilingual cities count in full for each language** — Montreal counts as a French city _and_ an English city, Brussels as French and Dutch, Hong Kong as Chinese and English, Singapore as English, Chinese, Malay and Tamil. Someone who speaks either language can work there, so the city is not split between them.
- **The ranking:** take the top 100 (or 200) cities by GDP per person, and count how many of them each language opens, and how many people live in them.

The data sources are listed under "Sources" at the end of this post.

- **Chinese** is second, but through Singapore, Hong Kong and Taiwan as much as through mainland China. Only Xiamen and Beijing from the mainland make the top 100.

Two kinds of language show up:

- **Concentrated languages** — Japanese, Korean, Turkish, German. Most of their big-city speakers live in one or two well-off cities, so P50 and P90 are close together.

For a learner, the average is not the useful number. What matters is the end of the range you are aiming for.

## What was wrong with the country version

Our [February post](/en/blog/2025-02-24-language-gdp-coverage) put each country's whole GDP under one language. Apart from the main problem — a country is too big a unit — it had these errors:

- **French was undercounted.** The table said French covered France, part of Canada and part of Belgium, but its $3.36T total was France alone. Quebec, Wallonia and Brussels, French-speaking Switzerland and French-speaking Africa were left out, which is why French ended up below Hindi.
- **Canada was counted twice in spirit:** all of its GDP went to English, while the table also listed it under French.
- **The cumulative chart used a different order from the ranking table.** It added Japanese before German and Hindi before Spanish, so the "7 languages cover 75%" milestones did not follow from the table.
- **India was counted entirely as Hindi**, even though the post itself said English is India's main business language — and most Indians do not speak Hindi as their first language.
- **The dates didn't match:** a post dated February 2025 cited IMF data from October 2025.

We have added a note to the old post pointing here.

## Limits of this ranking

- **GDP per person is not salary.** It counts everything a city produces — including company profits and, in some cities, oil and mining. Pekanbaru and Samarinda (Indonesia) and Ahwaz (Iran) rank high because of oil, coal and gas, not because ordinary jobs there pay well. Dublin's figure is inflated by multinationals booking profits in Ireland.
- **The UK figures come from the OECD instead.** The city database's GDP numbers put every British city at about half of what the OECD reports (London $38k instead of $72k), while other countries agreed within about 10%. We used the OECD's 2020 figures for London, Manchester, Leeds, Liverpool and Birmingham.
- **City boundaries are drawn by built-up area.** Guangzhou's urban centre includes Shenzhen, Dongguan and Foshan as one 41-million-person city, which pulls Shenzhen's high value down. Some rich cities have fewer than 1 million people in their dense core and are not in the list at all — Zurich, Geneva, Frankfurt and Luxembourg among them.
- **The data is from 2020**, a pandemic year, and the GDP map behind it is modelled from regional statistics rather than measured city by city.
- **Language assignments are judgement calls.** For example, we counted English in Indian, Pakistani, Philippine, Nigerian and Gulf cities, where it is a main working language, but not in Amsterdam or Stockholm, even though many jobs there are in English. Change one, and the ranking shifts with it.

## What this means for learners

1. **English first** — it opens about half of the world's richest big cities on its own.
2. **Then choose by where you would actually live.** For the very top tier, German, Arabic (the Gulf), French and Japanese each open several cities. For sheer numbers of well-off cities, Chinese and Spanish win.
3. **Bilingual cities give you two ways in.** Montreal, Brussels, Hong Kong, Singapore, Barcelona and Miami are places where your second language is a real advantage, not just a bonus.

Whatever you choose, getting to working fluency takes years of steady review. That is what [Lianki](/en) is for: it schedules every word and phrase you save so you review it just before you would forget it.
