# Games Where Temperature Matters: Frostpunk, Oxygen Not Included and More

_Posted October 18, 2026 by [lianki.com](https://lianki.com)_

Plenty of games have seasons: the ground turns white, the music changes, and maybe your crops stop growing. That is not what this post is about.

The games below make temperature a number you have to keep thinking about. Food spoils because a room got warm. A machine breaks because the pipe next to it carried hot water. Your character freezes because the wind picked up. You can't solve the cold once and forget it — it touches building, planning, clothing and combat all at once.

They are grouped roughly from "temperature is the whole game" to "temperature is one important system among many."

---

## Temperature is the simulation

### 1. Oxygen Not Included

- **Developer:** Klei Entertainment
- **Link:** [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/457140/Oxygen_Not_Included/)

![Oxygen Not Included: a colony dug into the asteroid](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/457140/ss_78d1c92edeecc7b17cafa9248867fe7d4390a0a0.600x338.jpg)
_Screenshot: Steam store page_

You run a colony of small "duplicants" inside an asteroid, and nearly everything in it has a temperature: the gas in each tile, the water in the pipes, the rock in the walls, the buildings themselves. Heat moves between them.

That makes heat a problem you engineer around. According to the community wiki, most buildings start taking damage once they pass their overheat temperature — 75 °C for most of them — and the material you build with can raise that limit a lot. Machines produce heat while they work, so a busy base slowly cooks itself unless you move that heat somewhere else. The wiki also points out that the game is not a strict physics model (some machines output more heat than their power use would explain), but it is detailed enough that "where does the heat go?" becomes a question you ask about every build.

### 2. Stationeers

- **Developer:** RocketWerkz
- **Link:** [Stationeers Wiki](https://stationeers-wiki.com/Atmosphere/en)

![Stationeers: inside a station](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/544550/67c6f761aa8335bf3f5e1192f887deb7da59a817/ss_67c6f761aa8335bf3f5e1192f887deb7da59a817.600x338.jpg)
_Screenshot: Steam store page_

Stationeers is a space-station builder with a gas simulation underneath. Every grid cell of air holds a certain amount of gas and a certain amount of heat energy; its temperature comes from the two together, and pressure depends on both. When two spaces connect, gas and heat flow until they even out, and each pipe network acts as one shared atmosphere.

So heating a small room is quick, a big one is slow, and raising the temperature of a sealed tank raises its pressure. The wiki notes that canisters can burst from pressure and that fuel can ignite by itself when it is hot or pressurized enough — temperature mistakes end in explosions.

### 3. Space Station 13

- **Developer:** open-source community (many "forks" maintained by different servers)
- **Link:** [Guide to Atmospherics — BeeStation wiki](https://wiki.beestation13.com/view/O2)

Space Station 13 is a multiplayer role-playing game on a space station, and one of its jobs is the atmospheric technician. Gases have different heat capacities, and fires need oxygen: a room full of plasma alone won't burn, but plasma mixed with oxygen will, and more oxygen makes it burn hotter. A bad plasma fire can heat a whole section of the station, and players fight it by cutting off oxygen, building firebreaks or cooling the room. Rules differ between servers, since each fork changes the code.

## Temperature shapes how you build

### 4. Frostpunk and Frostpunk 2

- **Developer:** 11 bit studios
- **Link:** [Frostpunk on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/323190/Frostpunk/)

![Frostpunk: a city built in rings around the generator](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/323190/ss_28db92509d505c855b07d480d749119fc147c84c.600x338.jpg)
_Screenshot: Steam store page_

Frostpunk is a city builder set in a frozen world, and the city's heart is a coal generator. The generator heats a circle around it, so the shape of the city is the shape of its heat: homes, workshops and hospitals all want to be inside that circle. Steam hubs extend the heat to buildings further out.

The weather keeps getting worse. Guides describe each 10 °C drop in outside temperature as lowering a building's heat level by one step, while insulation and generator upgrades raise it. People who spend their days in cold buildings get sick, so a cold snap is also a health crisis and a coal-supply crisis at the same time.

### 5. RimWorld

- **Developer:** Ludeon Studios
- **Link:** [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/294100/RimWorld/)

![RimWorld: a colony in the snow](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/294100/788bf7f8f71014b2be28b3dfa11405a06a7fb49e/ss_788bf7f8f71014b2be28b3dfa11405a06a7fb49e.600x338.jpg)
_Screenshot: Steam store page_

RimWorld is a colony simulator, and every room has its own temperature. The RimWorld Wiki lists what depends on it:

- **Food.** Warm food rots; cold slows rotting, and at 0 °C or below food stops spoiling. So most colonies build a freezer.
- **People.** Too much heat or cold leads to heatstroke or hypothermia, which can kill.
- **Machines.** A cooler moves heat from one side of a wall to the other — so its hot side has to vent somewhere harmless, ideally outdoors. Heat also leaks through walls and open doors.

A freezer next to a bedroom, a cooler blowing into the wrong room, a door left open in winter — small layout choices turn into temperature problems.

### 6. Factorio: Space Age — the planet Aquilo

- **Developer:** Wube Software
- **Link:** [Friday Facts #432 — Aquilo](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-432)

![Factorio: machines frozen on Aquilo](https://cdn.factorio.com/assets/blog-sync/fff-432-frozen.png)
_Image: Factorio Friday Facts #432_

Factorio is a factory-building game, and its Space Age expansion adds several planets. On the icy planet Aquilo, the developers write, most things freeze if they are not heated — machines, inserters, belts and pipes included. You bring heat with heating towers that burn fuel and spread it with heat pipes.

Everything you know about factory layout changes. Every machine has to stay close to a heat source, and players on the Steam forums complain that heat pipes take up a lot of space and that the middle lane of a wide belt line can freeze.

### 7. Dwarf Fortress

- **Developer:** Bay 12 Games
- **Link:** [Dwarf Fortress Wiki — Magma-safe](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/Magma-safe)

![Dwarf Fortress: a fortress map](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/975370/72780d67585fda02ff95373c9cfc0a7b35193341/ss_72780d67585fda02ff95373c9cfc0a7b35193341.600x338.jpg)
_Screenshot: Steam store page_

In Dwarf Fortress, materials have melting points measured in the game's own unit, degrees Urist (°U). Magma sits at 12000 °U, so whether something survives contact with magma comes down to one comparison: is its melting point higher? Pumps, floodgates and forges near magma must be made of "magma-safe" materials — iron and steel work, plenty of other things don't. A mistake here can melt part of your fortress's machinery.

## Temperature is your body

### 8. The Long Dark

- **Developer:** Hinterland Studio
- **Link:** [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/305620/The_Long_Dark/)

![The Long Dark: walking through snow](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/305620/ss_6e8604e40c58acd67071bb2e1a13f22a1f9fd288.600x338.jpg)
_Screenshot: Steam store page_

The Long Dark is a survival game in the Canadian wilderness after a mysterious disaster. Its key number is the "Feels Like" temperature: the developer's patch notes describe it as the air temperature minus the warmth of the clothes you are wearing, and it controls how fast your Cold meter drains. Wind chill makes it feel colder, clothing has a separate windproof rating, and frostbite is a separate risk again. Choosing whether to cross an open lake in a blizzard is mostly a temperature calculation.

### 9. The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom

- **Developer:** Nintendo
- **Link:** [Zelda Wiki — Temperature](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Temperature)

A thermometer on screen shows whether Link needs cold resistance, heat resistance or fire protection. Snowy mountains call for warm clothing or spicy food, the desert calls for cooling, and near the volcano Link and his wooden gear can burst into flames without fireproof protection. Temperature decides which outfit you wear, which meals you cook and which route you take.

### 10. Don't Starve Together

- **Developer:** Klei Entertainment
- **Link:** [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/322330/Dont_Starve_Together/)

![Don't Starve Together: winter](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/322330/ss_37e30c4cf8c945f1958890c8fa7346af6bea547d.600x338.jpg)
_Screenshot: Steam store page_

Yes, Don't Starve has seasons — but they are only the start. Your character has a body temperature, and winter means freezing while summer means overheating. The key item is the Thermal Stone: warm it next to a fire and it keeps you warm on the move; cool it and it keeps you cool. It wears out with use, and it stacks with warm or cooling clothing, so every trip away from base starts with "is my stone charged?"

### 11. Icarus

- **Developer:** RocketWerkz
- **Link:** [Icarus Wiki — Game Mechanics](https://icarus.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Mechanics)

![Icarus: a prospector in fur armor](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/1149460/ss_575ee577c444afc65f8bbf8f7f18d705eacbca15.600x338.jpg)
_Screenshot: Steam store page_

In Icarus each biome has its own temperature range, and armor gives cold or heat resistance that widens the range you can stand. The developers' "Scorched and Frozen" update made gear and food affect your internal body temperature directly, and added heat exhaustion and heat stroke alongside the existing cold effects. Packing for an Arctic trip and packing for the desert are different jobs.

## Temperature is your machine

### 12. BattleTech and MechWarrior

- **Developer (BattleTech, 2018):** Harebrained Schemes
- **Link:** [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/637090/BATTLETECH/)

![BattleTech: a turn-based mech battle](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/637090/ss_7552f834224513ed871017e70f951ced1eeea62f.600x338.jpg)
_Screenshot: Steam store page_

In the BattleTech universe, firing weapons heats up your giant robot ("mech"). Heat sinks remove it, and energy weapons tend to run the hottest. Fire too much in one turn and the mech overheats — taking internal damage, and at the limit shutting down. Each turn is a choice between firing everything now and staying cool enough to keep fighting.

### 13. Elite Dangerous

- **Developer:** Frontier Developments
- **Link:** [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/359320/Elite_Dangerous/)

![Elite Dangerous: a ship near a star](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/359320/ss_00b2ffcc1a62cb236ca198a4f7702d57890e0d14.600x338.jpg)
_Screenshot: Steam store page_

Your spaceship builds up heat. "Silent running" closes the vents so the ship is harder to detect, but the heat stays inside and keeps climbing, so you can't keep it on forever. Players launch heat sinks to dump heat, and flying too close to a star while jumping can overheat the ship. Stealth and heat are the same resource.

### 14. Kerbal Space Program

- **Developer:** Squad
- **Link:** [KSP forum — re-entry heating](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/161085-re-entry-heating-mystery)

![Kerbal Space Program: a crew capsule in orbit](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/220200/ss_395fa5eeac33f83a3f80cfabfe221899d41e21d0.600x338.jpg)
_Screenshot: Steam store page_

Bringing a rocket home means getting through re-entry. Parts heat up as they hit the atmosphere and are destroyed if they pass their limit, so you need a heat shield in front — and only what sits behind it is protected. Players describe the trade-off: a steep re-entry is hotter but shorter, a shallow one is cooler but heats you for longer.

## What these games have in common

Temperature is a hidden number. You can't see it unless you check, it changes slowly, and by the time it becomes obvious — food rotting, a machine melting, a colonist with hypothermia — it is too late to fix cheaply. The players who do well build habits: check the freezer, check the stone, check the thermometer before crossing the lake.

Memory works much the same way. How well you remember a word is also a hidden number that drops slowly over time, and you usually find out it hit zero only when you need the word and it's gone. Lianki's [FSRS scheduler](/en/blog/2025-01-15-fsrs-algorithm) does the checking for you: it estimates how well you still remember each card and brings it back just before you would forget, the way a good thermostat turns on the heat before the pipes freeze.
